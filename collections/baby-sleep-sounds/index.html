Read before playing

Use sound cautiously around infants

  1. Keep the device well away from the crib. Never place a phone, speaker or cords in the baby's sleep space or attach a sound machine to the crib.
  2. Use the lowest practical volume. Screen volume percentages and MP3 loudness do not measure the sound pressure at a baby's ears. If in doubt, use less sound.
  3. Limit listening time. Consider turning it off once the baby is asleep. The optional Sleep Timer is a convenience, not a guaranteed safety cutoff when a phone is locked or backgrounded.

Based on the American Academy of Pediatrics' guidance on noise exposure and its safe-sleep recommendations.

Practical use

A simple, cautious listening routine

01

Prepare the sleep space

Follow standard safe-sleep practices: place an infant on their back on a firm, flat sleep surface with a fitted sheet, and keep loose objects and cords outside the sleep space. Audio does not replace these measures.

02

Position and check the speaker

Put the device away from the crib, not under bedding or within reach. Check the actual volume in the room before playing. There is no universal safe percentage that a website can set for you.

03

Keep the session brief

If you use sound at all, select a short timer or stop it manually once it is no longer needed. A browser-based Sleep Timer can be delayed while the phone is suspended, so do not depend on it as the only control.

Why the MP3 duration is not the listening duration

Soft White Noise and Pink Noise each use a ten-minute synthesized master; Rain on Window is a ten-minute arrangement derived from about one minute of CC0 field recording. Native HLS or MP3 looping can repeat these sources. That does not add new independent recording, certify a sound pressure level, or make long exposure automatically safe.

For parents and caregivers

Baby sleep sounds: questions and answers

Is white noise necessary for a baby to sleep?

No. Some families use background noise, but it is optional and not a requirement for healthy sleep. A quiet environment may be the better choice. No track on this site is guaranteed to help a particular baby fall asleep.

How loud should baby sleep sounds be?

As low as practical, with the device placed away from the baby. A number on a phone's volume slider or an audio file's LUFS value cannot establish the sound-pressure level at the baby's ears. See the AAP noise guidance.

Should I play sounds all night?

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends limiting the duration of infant sleep-machine use. This site can loop audio continuously for other listening purposes, but its ability to loop is not a recommendation for overnight infant exposure. Consider a brief session or turn it off when the baby is asleep.

Where should I put the phone or speaker?

Keep the sound device as far from the infant as possible and never in or attached to the crib. Keep electrical cables and other loose objects out of the baby's sleep area. Follow AAP safe-sleep guidance regardless of whether you use audio.

Are these sounds calibrated to a safe infant volume?

No. They are audio files, not clinically calibrated sound machines. Speaker amplification, distance, room acoustics and duration affect exposure, and the player cannot measure the sound level at a baby's ear. Caregivers must control actual output.
