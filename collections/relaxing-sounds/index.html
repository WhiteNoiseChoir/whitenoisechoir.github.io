Practical listening guide

How to build a comfortable listening session

Start quietly

Begin at a low device volume and increase only if needed. Headphones, phone speakers, and external speakers can sound very different at the same on-screen volume percentage.

Choose predictable or varied

For a changing scene, try rainfall, surf, or fireplace. For a more stationary sound, try pink or brown noise. The sound you enjoy is more useful than any promised “best frequency.”

Use the timer if it suits you

Set a Sleep Timer for a finite session, or leave it off for continuous listening. A backgrounded browser or operating system can still interrupt playback, and a timer is not a safety device.

Keep listening across pages

Once a track is playing, the site’s shared player stays available as you open Sound Pages or another collection. You can pause or stop it at any time.

About the recordings

Continuous playback does not mean a never-repeating field recording. Ocean Waves contains about 1:30 of natural material per cycle, Heavy Rain about 3:10, and Waterfall about 6:15. Rain on Window and Fireplace are ten-minute arrangements made from shorter CC0 recordings; the sound textures repeat. Original synthesized noise is marked separately. Common questions

Relaxing sounds — questions and answers

Which sound should I try first to unwind?

Start with a sound whose changes feel pleasant rather than distracting. Rain on Window is a gentler starting point; Ocean Waves has more movement. If you prefer an even texture, try Pink Noise. There is no universal winner.

Can I keep these sounds playing for hours?

Yes. The site uses a continuous native HLS timeline where supported and an MP3 loop elsewhere, with a player that keeps the session while browsing. Internet, power-saving and operating-system behavior can still interrupt audio.

Are all the sounds recordings from nature?

No. Rain, waves, waterfall and fireplace use licensed CC0 field recordings or arrangements. Gentle Wind, Pink Noise and Brown Noise are created digitally, and each Sound Page identifies its origin.

Do relaxing sounds treat stress or sleep problems?

No such outcome is promised. This collection is for personal listening preferences, not medical treatment. If sound is irritating or uncomfortable, turn it down or stop.
