Practical setup

For a comfortable night of listening

Place the speaker sensibly

Use a comfortable listening distance and avoid putting a speaker directly beside your ear. If you use headphones in bed, be mindful of pressure, cable and comfort while lying down.

Expect differences by device

White noise can sound crisp through small speakers, while low-heavy brown noise may lose some bass. A softer track on one device can sound quite different elsewhere.

Keep control of the session

The mini player remains available as you browse this site. Choose the timer, pause or stop. A locked phone may limit background tasks; the operating system can interrupt sound despite continuous HLS.

Know when to turn it off

Sound is optional. If the noise is irritating, too loud or keeps you awake, lower it or stop. Persistent sleep difficulties deserve more than a background audio track.

How continuous playback works

Each selected sound uses a ten-minute original DSP master at 44.1 kHz stereo, published as MP3 and a static, multi-cycle HLS stream for compatible browsers. The HLS timeline extends well beyond a night; it is not an infinite live broadcast and it repeats source material. Frequently asked

White noise for sleep: common questions

Is white noise or brown noise better for sleep?

No noise color has been established as universally best for sleep. White noise is broadband and often brighter. Brown noise emphasizes lower frequencies. Try both at a comfortable volume and keep whichever is less distracting for you.

Can I leave the sound playing all night?

The player supports continuous playback and an optional sleep timer. On supported iPhone browsers, native HLS is intended to avoid a restart at each ten-minute boundary; other browsers use looping MP3. Internet, device power settings and operating-system behavior can still interrupt a session.

Does the ten-minute MP3 mean the sound stops after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes is the length of each synthesized source master, not the maximum listening session. The browser repeats the MP3 or plays an extended HLS timeline, depending on its audio support.

How loud should I play white noise at bedtime?

There is no universal safe device-volume percentage because speaker output and distance vary. Start low, avoid uncomfortable levels and do not rely on the sleep timer as a safety cutoff. This page is intended for adult listening, not infant sleep guidance.
