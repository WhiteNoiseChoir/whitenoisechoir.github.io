What does Deep Brown Noise sound like?

Standard Brown Noise is typically modeled around −20 dB per frequency decade. The measured slope of this Deep Brown master is substantially steeper, so it should be understood as an intentionally extreme filtered bass texture, not a standardized new noise color.

How it differs from related sounds

Understanding Deep Brown Noise

Why this is more extreme than textbook Brown Noise

Ideal Brown (Brownian or red) Noise has a power-spectrum slope of approximately −20 dB per decade. This separately filtered Deep Brown master measures about −66.42 dB per decade across the analysis range. That is much steeper than the standard power law, largely because the DSP shaping also filters away much of the higher-frequency content. The design applies strong low-frequency emphasis with a high-frequency roll-off around 550 Hz. It is a custom bass-heavy texture, not a recognized new spectral standard.

That distinction affects real listening. The master can be difficult to hear on a small phone speaker with little bass output. Increasing the phone volume to compensate is not a reliable way to judge it and can become uncomfortable when changing speakers or switching to brighter noise. A larger speaker may reveal the rumble more clearly without requiring the audio to be re-rendered.

Brown Noise, Deep Brown and Low Rumble

Use Brown Noise as the conventional reference and Low Rumble for a separate synthetic hum-like character. This Deep Brown master has its own full-length file and measured five-band profile; it is not Brown Noise merely turned down. Compare at a gentle starting volume on the same device. It has no guaranteed sleep or stress-relief effect.

For conventional spectral definitions, see IEEE's colored-noise overview and the American Academy of Audiology's listening-volume caution. These are references for the physical principles, not endorsements of the recording.

Questions about Deep Brown Noise

Is Deep Brown Noise just Brown Noise made louder?

No. Its measured spectral distribution is much more heavily weighted toward low frequencies; increasing volume alone would not create that change.

Why does it sound quiet through my phone?

Small built-in speakers roll off deep bass. A lower perceived level on one device does not mean the source file is silent or defective.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.

Is Deep Brown Noise scientifically defined as −66.42 dB per decade?

No. That is the measured fitted slope of this particular DSP master, not a universal definition. Standard ideal Brown Noise is closer to −20 dB per decade.

Why do headphones and phones sound so different?

Many small speakers attenuate low frequencies, while larger headphones or speakers can reproduce more of the bass. Do not compensate by turning the volume up sharply.
