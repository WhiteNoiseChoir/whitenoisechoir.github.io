The sound of real rainfall

What makes this Heavy Rain recording different?

This track is taken from a real rainfall field recording credited to MikeNavajas, rather than a synthetic rain generator. The amount and texture of rain can change as the original recording progresses. Unlike a uniform colored-noise file, real rainfall contains individual impacts and fluctuations, so the background may be more noticeable at certain moments.

Compared with Rain on Window, this recording is intended for a denser rainfall background. Rain on Window is a constructed ten-minute arrangement from about one minute of original raindrop material. Heavy Rain uses 189.76 seconds of independent recorded rain before the next loop. Both are honest CC0-derived sources, but they differ in density and repetition interval.

How to use it without surprises

Try it while reading, taking a quiet break or setting up a bedtime atmosphere. If a passing change in the rain catches your attention, compare the calmer window sound or a steadier Pink Noise master. Start at a low volume; a percent on the player does not establish a safe sound pressure level at your ears.

Frequently asked

Questions about Heavy Rain Sound

Does this heavy rain sound include a full thunderstorm?

This is a rainfall recording, not a dedicated thunderstorm or lightning-effects track. Do not expect a timed sequence of thunder effects; natural recording details can vary.

Is the audio a ten-minute rain recording?

No. The independently captured source used for this master is 189.76 seconds (roughly 3 minutes 10 seconds). A continuous HLS playlist or looping MP3 repeats the recorded material for a longer session.

Can I keep it playing while I open other sounds?

Yes. The shared WhiteNoiseChoir player can retain a session while you browse supported pages. Choose the optional Sleep Timer to end playback automatically when supported, but device power management can still interrupt sound or delay a timer.

Should I choose Heavy Rain or Rain on Window?

Heavy Rain is the fuller rain option with a longer independent recording cycle. Rain on Window has lighter, more recognizable drops against glass but uses shorter material in an edited ten-minute arrangement. Try both at low volume.
