Understanding Blue Noise

What the blue-noise definition actually means

In acoustics, an ideal blue-noise power spectral density rises in proportion to frequency. That is an approximate +10 dB per tenfold frequency increase, not a fixed musical pitch. Our independent 600-second DSP master measures +10.01 dB per decade over the reported 120–6,000 Hz analysis region. The steepening energy distribution creates a clear high-frequency hiss compared with a relatively flat white-noise reference.

Blue noise also appears in graphics and digital dithering discussions, but a spatial blue-noise pattern in an image is not this sound file. This page is specifically about audible colored noise generated for playback. Its brightness is a spectral property, not evidence of better sleep, improved concentration or hearing treatment.

Blue Noise versus Violet Noise

Violet Noise has a stronger high-frequency weighting, measuring about +20.01 dB per decade in our master. Blue is the less steep of the two: if violet sounds excessively sharp, blue is not as treble-dominant, though it can still be uncomfortable when played loudly. Try White Noise for a more even spectral reference or Pink Noise for less treble emphasis. Lower the volume before switching files because they are not perceptually loudness-matched.

For conventional spectral definitions, see IEEE's colored-noise overview and the American Academy of Audiology's listening-volume caution. These are references for the physical principles, not endorsements of the recording.

Questions about Blue Noise

Is Blue Noise brighter than White Noise?

Yes, in the idealized spectral sense: Blue Noise puts proportionally more energy at higher frequencies. Perceived loudness still depends on the device and volume.

Should I leave Blue Noise playing all night?

There is no general reason to assume bright noise is suitable for everyone. Keep the volume low, listen for discomfort, and choose a gentler texture if preferred.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.

Is audio Blue Noise the same as blue-noise dithering?

No. Blue noise is also discussed as a spatial distribution for image dithering, but this page plays a time-varying audible noise signal with a rising power spectrum. The media and use case are different.

Can Blue Noise repair speakers or treat tinnitus?

No. The audio is neither a speaker-cleaner nor a clinically calibrated treatment. Treble-weighted signals can be uncomfortable when loud; start low and stop if bothered.
