What makes this recording distinctive?

Recorded by orlandorizo with a Zoom H5N alongside the Magdalena River at Los Dinamos south of Mexico City. This independent stereo CC0 field recording has a forceful flow and a broad continuous rush. It is not the same file as the existing Waterfall.

The Magdalena River produces a stronger current than the separate Jadwiżanka forest stream recording. The existing Waterfall is a denser, falling-water wash. Shower is synthetic rather than a field recording.

Original field recording and CC0 license

Recorded by orlandorizo and released under Creative Commons Zero 1.0 (CC0). Read the original Freesound recording and licensing page. The approved WhiteNoiseChoir audio was made from the creator's publicly available lossy Freesound HQ MP3 preview, not the original WAV. Encoding at 192 kbps does not restore fidelity already lost.

Original source duration: approximately 924.7 seconds. Independently selected field source used: 605 seconds. Approved master: 600 seconds, 44.1 kHz, stereo, 192 kbps MP3. The same exact SHA-256 approved listening-review master was used in production without re-encoding.

How continuous playback works

The ten-minute master uses approximately 605 seconds of distinct recorded sound with a short circular seam; no separate short section is repeated within the first ten-minute arrangement. A seven-day static HLS VOD playlist repeats the whole 600-second audio cycle for long listening on native-HLS devices. Other browsers use MP3 loop fallback. The static playlist is not an infinite livestream, and iOS lock-screen behavior remains device-dependent.

Water surges can be louder than the average level. Local river surroundings may contain incidental background activity. Start at low volume. This is a listening aid, not a medical treatment. Keep volume comfortable, particularly around children and infants.
