Source-specific listening guide

Why simulated running shower water differs from rain or waterfall audio

A real shower sends many short water impacts onto tiles, a basin, skin and other nearby surfaces. Our source is a synthetic approximation of that fine spray-like texture, not a microphone recording from a bathroom. It uses independent broadband random material with a high-pass emphasis around 680 Hz, an upper-frequency cutoff near 10.5 kHz and gentle density modulation. Those are synthesis settings, not measurements of water pressure, showerhead design or room acoustics.

The approved decoded MP3 contains relatively little 80–250 Hz energy. Sampled band levels rise strongly through the middle and upper bands, unlike bass-rich Air Conditioner or Low Rumble sound. Its +15.69 dB-per-decade fitted slope across 120–6,000 Hz summarizes a rising portion of the file's spectrum, not a claim that all real showers share this slope. The near-flat top two measured bands help explain its sustained bright, fine-spray-like hiss.

For Rain on Window, the source arrangement comes from real rainfall material and can include separate droplet impacts against glass. Heavy Rain uses an approved independent CC0 rainfall field recording of 189.76 seconds. Waterfall uses an independently recorded 375.48-second CC0 source. Shower has none of those environmental capture details: it is deliberately even and continuously synthesized for a different, indoor running-water concept.

The differences should be assessed by pressing the A/B choices, not by reading the track names alone. A bright noise spectrum does not guarantee that the sound convincingly recreates your bathroom. Headphones and speakers alter perceived brightness, and these samples are not normalized for equal perceived loudness. Lower your volume before switching among tracks.

What you are hearing

The sound is a self-contained, ten-minute digital master shaped to evoke consistent water spray. It is not a faucet leak detector, water-quality monitor or measurement of plumbing performance. It also contains no guarantee of convincing room reflections or realistic shower startup and stop events.

Choosing between a shower, rain and waterfall

Choose the shower simulation if you want an indoor, relatively consistent hiss-like water texture. Choose Rain on Window if individual rainfall contact is important. Heavy Rain provides a fuller outdoor rainfall recording, while Waterfall has a real, continuously flowing outdoor-water source. Their natural source lengths differ, and playback repetition does not increase the amount of original recording.

Useful listening habits

The track can be played as ambient background when relaxing or reading, with Sleep Timer available in the shared player. Questions about Shower — Synthetic

Was this recorded in a bathroom?

No. Shower is an original synthesized approximation of water noise, not a recorded fixture.

Why does it sound brighter than some other water sounds?

Its measured frequency distribution places more energy toward upper frequencies. Device response and volume can change how sharp it feels.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.

No. The 600-second audio source is original synthetic DSP. No shower fixture or bathroom was recorded.
