Understanding Neutral Mid Noise

Why a near-flat slope does not make this White Noise

The measured slope across 120–6,000 Hz is approximately −2.76 dB per decade, much closer to flat than several other variants. However, a single fitted line hides individual differences across frequency bands. This source was generated with its own mid-band-oriented filtering, including a stronger low-frequency high-pass stage around 680 Hz and upper filtering around 4,500 Hz. The per-band measurements below show what that combination actually produced.

“Neutral Mid Noise” is a site-specific name, not an internationally standardized spectral category. It does not mean that listeners should hear the entire midrange as equally loud, and it does not imply a specific medical or cognitive effect. This independent source may offer a different texture from ordinary White Noise despite superficially similar overall slope numbers.

Do not confuse it with Green Noise

Our separate Green Noise candidate has an explicitly declared 165 Hz high-pass and 1,550 Hz low-pass and is still awaiting listening approval. It is not a rename or an approved replacement for Neutral Mid Noise. Until the green candidate passes review, use the independently approved White, Pink or Blue files as reference comparisons; this page itself remains noindex pending a clearer standalone search intent.

For conventional spectral definitions, see IEEE's colored-noise overview and the American Academy of Audiology's listening-volume caution. These are references for the physical principles, not endorsements of the recording.

Questions about Neutral Mid Noise

Is Neutral Mid Noise a recognized acoustic standard?

No. It is a WhiteNoiseChoir descriptive name for this original DSP master.

Does it have the same band-pass filters as Green Noise?

No. Green Noise is a separate master with declared filter cutoffs. We do not attribute those settings to this file.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.

Is Neutral Mid Noise an official color of noise?

No. It is a descriptive WhiteNoiseChoir name for an independently rendered DSP master with its own band filtering. Its measured response matters more than the label.

Is Neutral Mid Noise a substitute for Green Noise?

No. The Green Noise candidate has different documented filters and is not yet approved for listening. The two files are distinct, and neither label is a universally standardized spectrum.
