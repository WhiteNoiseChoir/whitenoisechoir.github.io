Source-specific listening guide

What this air-conditioner-like hum contains—and what it does not

An actual air conditioner may have changes in fan speed, compressor cycling, vibration through walls and the character of a particular room. Our track contains none of those verified observations. It is an independently generated 600-second synthetic room-machine ambience constructed from filtered noise and a quiet 60 Hz component with a 120 Hz harmonic. These tones are synthesis parameters, not measurements of a real compressor or evidence about the electrical system in a user's country.

The measured decoded MP3 puts comparatively more energy at the low end of the displayed frequency ranges. Its 5,000–10,000 Hz sampled band is far below the lower-frequency bands, which is consistent with a subdued, low-pass-shaped texture. The −45.25 dB/decade fitted slope covers the stated 120–6,000 Hz analysis region, not every frequency in the master or any other air-conditioning unit.

An important limitation: our five-band spectral-shape screen found this source close to Warm Pink Noise (1.91 dB RMSE after normalizing each track against its first band). That is a prompt for careful listening, not a proof that they sound identical. The AC render has additional low-level harmonic and amplitude details, while Warm Pink is a differently shaped synthetic noise variant. Publishing two pages as distinct search results solely because their labels differ would be difficult to justify.

Unlike Desk Fan, which emphasizes a small-fan-like presentation, this track is organized as a steadier cooling-system backdrop. There is no thermostat interaction or predictable compressor cycle to hear. Unlike Low Rumble, the AC concept includes a quiet machine-like tonal element; whether that element is audible depends on playback hardware and listening level.

For rooms, reading and sleep routines

The player can provide an unobtrusive continuous room-hum texture without a physical air conditioner running. It cannot cool a room, reduce humidity or replace ventilation. If you use it near sleep time, keep your device comfortable and reduce volume as needed; the track is not medically validated as a sleep aid.

Why we are not calling it an air-conditioner recording

No physical AC unit, manufacturer, fan speed or room geometry was sampled. The published track is original DSP material, and the in-page AudioObject points to that exact MP3. Listening similarity is a subjective quality question separate from source provenance.

Questions about Air Conditioner — Synthetic

Is this an actual AC recording?

No. This recording is independently synthesized. We do not claim to have captured a physical air conditioner.

Does it include realistic compressor on/off cycles?

It is a continuous background texture, not a verified model-specific sequence of compressor starts or stops.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.

Will this audio imitate an AC compressor turning on and off?

No. The source is intentionally continuous and does not contain a verified compressor duty cycle or thermostat switching sequence.

Why does it resemble Warm Pink Noise in the spectral screen?

Both concentrate much of their measured energy at lower frequencies. The 1.91 dB five-band screening score reflects broad spectral shape only; their distinct synthesis details require listening to evaluate.
