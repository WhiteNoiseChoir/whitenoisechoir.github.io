What does Warm Pink Noise sound like?

Ideal Pink Noise has a slope close to −10 dB per frequency decade. The measured Warm Pink master is considerably more low-heavy, so the label is a perceptual description of this variant rather than a claim of textbook 1/f noise.

How it differs from related sounds

If you need a scientifically conventional Pink Noise reference, use our standard Pink Noise page. Warm Pink is better treated as an alternate bass-weighted listening option, with its true measured characteristics stated below.

Choosing a listening situation

Try it when standard Pink Noise feels too bright, comparing at a comfortable level. A low-heavy spectrum can reproduce differently on earbuds, laptop speakers and a larger speaker.

Questions about Warm Pink Noise

Is Warm Pink Noise the same thing as standard Pink Noise?

No. Its measured spectral tilt differs substantially from the conventional 1/f curve; it is a custom tonal variation.

Can I compare them using the same device volume?

You can, but the tracks are not automatically loudness-matched and equal percentage settings do not guarantee equal perceived levels.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.
