The acoustic character of Pink Noise

Pink Noise is often described as equal energy per octave. Its idealized power spectral density falls approximately as 1/f, equivalent to a decline of around 3 dB per octave, or 10 dB per tenfold frequency increase. That is why it typically sounds less sharp than White Noise but not as dark as Brown Noise.

Every speaker and headphone has its own frequency response. Listening on a phone may make the lower frequencies less prominent, so you should compare at a comfortable level rather than compensating with high volume.

Pink Noise — Frequently asked questions

Is Pink Noise quieter than White Noise?

Not necessarily. Pink Noise emphasizes less high-frequency energy, so it may sound softer or less hiss-like, but the actual loudness depends on the master level, playback device and volume. The files are not automatically loudness-matched.

What is meant by equal energy per octave?

In the idealized model, each frequency band spanning a doubling of frequency carries approximately the same power. It does not mean each individual hertz is equally strong; that describes White Noise.

Does Pink Noise help with sleep or focus?

Some listeners enjoy it while winding down or working, but personal responses vary. Do not interpret audio preferences as proven treatment for insomnia, concentration disorders or other medical conditions.

Does this recording contain voices, music or nature sounds?

No. The main Pink Noise master is synthesized noise, not a recording of weather, speech, or music. Distinct rain and fan recordings are in other library categories.

Will the sound stop at minute ten?

No. Ten minutes describes the independent master file. Continuous listening repeats that source until you stop, the timer runs out, or the device interrupts playback.
