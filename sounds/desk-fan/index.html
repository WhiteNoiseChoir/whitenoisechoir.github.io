Source-specific listening guide

The mechanical character of this simulated desk fan

A tabletop fan typically combines airflow turbulence, motor vibration and some blade-related modulation. This track is an interpretation of that texture, not a recording of a fan operating in a room. Its generator shapes broadband noise, adds a quiet 112 Hz synthetic motor component with a harmonic, and applies gentle modulation. These are deliberate synthesis choices; they do not establish the blade speed, electrical frequency or acoustic output of any specific appliance.

In the approved decoded MP3, the 80–250 Hz and 250–750 Hz analysis bands are close in sampled level. The high-frequency end declines markedly above 5 kHz. This explains why the sound has a relatively soft, mechanical background rather than the bright hiss of the Shower simulation. The measured −28.59 dB-per-decade fit describes part of this file, not a general fan-noise formula.

The real Box Fan recording is preferable if you want genuine environmental artifacts and motor detail. That CC0 source contains 67.52 seconds of independent recording before repeat; this independently generated desk-fan master contains 600 seconds of distinct rendered material. Longer does not automatically mean more realistic. A useful comparison is to listen for small fluctuations in airflow, identifiable motor detail and how obvious the loop boundary feels.

The Airplane Cabin and Desk Fan masters share some broad-band spectral shape in our five-band screening, despite different synthesizer profiles. That screen is not a listener identity test. The two files include different tonal and modulation settings, but users should audition them before assuming they deliver sharply different experiences.

Questions about Desk Fan — Synthetic

Was this sound recorded from a real desk fan?

No. It is an original synthesized fan-like approximation; the source label is deliberately transparent.

Why can a synthetic fan sound feel more even?

The DSP is not constrained by room reflections and incidental environmental sounds in a real field recording. Realism still requires subjective listening approval.

Does the sound stop after ten minutes?

No. Ten minutes describes the MP3 master; the player uses continuous HLS or MP3 looping until stopped, the timer expires, or your device interrupts playback. HLS repeats the master rather than creating additional independent audio.

Can this reproduce the sound of a particular fan model?

No. The tonal and airflow components are generated by code, with no measured make/model profile. A real recording remains the right choice for appliance-specific sound.

Why does the real Box Fan page mention a shorter duration?

That file has 67.52 seconds of independently recorded CC0 material and is looped during playback. This synthetic page uses a separate, independently rendered 600-second file; both can play beyond their source durations.
