Why do Ocean Waves feel different from steady noise?

Surf tends to form repeating swells, louder breaking moments and quieter spaces in between. This is a real CC0 wave recording credited to florianreichelt rather than a simulated water-noise filter. The precise shape of each swell comes from the recording, not from a generated sine wave or noise-color preset.

For people who enjoy gentle change during a quiet break, those ebbs and flows may be appealing. They can also draw attention away from reading if you prefer an even background. Compare with Waterfall, which creates a more sustained wash, or Pink Noise, an original DSP source with a much more constant statistical character.

Know exactly what repeats

The edited master contains 89.83 seconds of independent wave recording per cycle. The original Freesound file is labeled “Waves of Hawaii”; that is a source title, not a claim that WhiteNoiseChoir recorded the location. Our player extends a listening session by repeating the approved source, and does not promise hours of unique ocean capture.

Frequently asked

Questions about Ocean Waves Sounds

Are the waves a real field recording?

Yes. The original author is florianreichelt, published under CC0. The original page is linked in the provenance section and the edited master is labeled separately.

Why is the source only about 90 seconds?

This was the accessible source material for the approved edit. The MP3 contains 89.83 seconds of independently recorded surf; HLS and MP3 looping repeat that cycle for longer listening.

Is ocean surf quieter than waterfall audio?

Not necessarily. Actual playback level depends on mastering, speaker response and user volume. The ocean has a more wave-like rise-and-fall rhythm, while the waterfall texture is more continuously rushing.

What if I prefer fewer changes in sound?

Try Pink Noise or another consistently rendered noise-color master. You can compare sounds directly, then keep the player active while browsing.
